No. 001

How we grow individuals in our head

Why do we learn English. If you asked this question a few years ago, the basic consensus was that English is a language used worldwide, therefore you should learn it to be able to obtain opportunities to input diverse information. However with the rapid improvement of technology, this reasoning is starting to slowly disappear. For instance, recent inventions enabled ear phones to translate many languages simultaneously while two individuals speak. Furthermore, the usage of AI as a translator is getting normalised around the world. This then takes away the incentive to learn not only English, but any other foreign language around the world.

I for one, didn't believe this to be true, but the reasoning I had behind this assumption was unclear to me. My emotions of wanting the effort I took to advance my english to be evaluated was getting in the way of logical reasoning.

This is when I looked back at the original idea. Multi language education is not needed, because we can translate the languages even if we can't fluently use them. I realised that this idea only stands if the act of learning new languages gives you only the benefit of being able to use them. I believe this to not be true.

For example, if we were to look at a portrait of a woman, how do we process the information we are presented with. First, we process it through our senses. A woman with long hair and a red dress. We know what these things are, therefore we can understand these things as concepts rather than complex thoughts. However, we also think beyond what we can see. Maybe the woman in the painting is sad, happy, grieving, or celebrating. These thoughts are different to the initial one in a sense where we process the information first, and we articulate it through our language.

In this way, I feel that complex thought would not be possible without language. Language is what enables us to differentiate one concept from another, and because we can differentiate it, we can articulate it and turn it into a train of complex thoughts. Unless we cannot recognise the information through our senses, language is the only thing that makes them understandable.

If Japanese had a word, a label for a specific piece of information, and english lacked an equivalent term for it, that would mean that that piece of information is much more difficult to consciously articulate while using english in thought. It does not mean the concept would cease to exist just because you speak a different language, but rather that the piece of information may be experienced, but is not articulated or connected in our minds when using that specific language. So, to my understanding by learning a new language you can gain a label to explain a concept that you can then use to construct your thoughts.

These differences in labeling, different thought spans rooted in language exist because of the unique history and culture of each country. What they needed to label to survive, or what they deemed important enough to.

This, I feel is the core reasoning behind learning a different language. Not only to communicate, but to understand the world through a different lens in our minds. If a merely translation was chosen as a replacement for multilingual education, it may enable us to understand each other easier, but we will still be understanding it through our own languages lens. The act of imputing a different system of thought must be the central value in multi language education.

Words have power. To move one's heart.

Words are what make us beautifully human.
